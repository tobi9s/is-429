The theory I have selected for this assignment is \textbf{deterrence theory} as I believe that it is how I can explain compliant behaviour for my part or in that sense, why I am not keen on commiting non-compliant behaviour. As I would describe myself more as a non-confrontational type of person, I take inspiration from deterrence theory as way to explain, and by extension steer my behaviour as way to not commit actions that can be seen as "out-of-line" or non-compliant, be it either at work or in public \cite{}. One example for how deterrence theory drives me to comply with rules or norms is to lock my computer when I am physically absent. Back when I was working prior to starting my Master's, the company had an informal rule of locking your own computer when absent, e.g. when going to the toilet, getting coffee, etc. Since this rule was not formalized and there would be no formal punishment for not doing it, there was no problem when people actually did not lock their computers. The catch was that the individual teams in that company had their rules about handling unlocked computers while their owner was absent. The general rule of my team was when someone did not lock their computer while absent, we would either send a invitation to coffee to the entire team or flip their display so they could not work properly. 
\newline 
As I have seen instances of that happening to my co-workers on multiple occasions, this drove me to comply with that rule out of fear that I would have to either spend money on coffees or be extremly annoyed that I would spend five minutes fixing my display even though technically, I would have done nothing wrong. In this scenario, deterrence theory can be used to explain how I stayed compliant to this particular rule or in another way, how it forced me and my co-workers to comply out of fear of "punishment" \cite{}. There's also the other way around where I can use deterrence theory as a way to explain instances where I show non-compliant behaviour. Funnily, this instance happened quite recently as I got a speeding ticket  back at home in Germany and subsquently had to pay a fine - here, deterrence theory can also be used to explain why I was driving over the speed limit despite knowing that I would have to pay a fine if I were to be caught. This instance can be easily mapped to the core components of deterrence theory:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Certainty:} Even though it is certain that one would have to pay a fine if they were caught speeding, speeding cameras are not typically at the same place, leaving the risk of being caught somewhat uncertain. If I thought that a certain place did not have a speeding camera, I could not care less about speeding.
    \item \textbf{Severity:} Compared to Norway, Germany is very lax when it comes to speeding fines \cite{speeding}. As the severity of punishment is not high, it did not bother me if I was caught. But the caveat is that the severity was not that important to me as the certainty since the level of severity would not matter if it was certain that I would not be caught \cite{engel2015afraid}. 
    \item \textbf{Swiftness:} Considering that I received the fine despite being caught in August, the tardiness from being caught to punished was so strong that it also did not bother me because I had already forgotten.
\end{itemize}
