The theory I have selected for this assignment is \textbf{deterrence theory} as I believe that it is how I can explain compliant behaviour for my part or in that sense, why I am not keen on commiting non-compliant behaviour.
\newline
As I would describe myself more as a non-confrontational type of person, I take inspiration from deterrence theory as way to explain, and by extension steer my behaviour as way to self-regulate myself from commiting actions that can be seen as "out-of-line" or non-compliant, be it either at work or in public. 
\newline
One example for how deterrence theory drives me to comply with rules or norms is to lock my computer when I am physically absent. Back when I was working prior to starting my Master's, the company had an informal rule of locking your own computer when absent, e.g. when going to the toilet, getting coffee, etc. Since this rule was not formalized and there would be no formal punishment for not doing it, there was no problem when people actually did not lock their computers. 
\newline
The catch was that the individual teams in that company had their rules about handling unlocked computers while their owner was absent. The general rule of my team was when someone did not lock their computer while absent, we would either send a invitation to coffee to the entire team or flip their display so they could not work properly. 
\newline 
As I have seen instances of that happening to my co-workers on multiple occasions, this drove me to comply with that rule out of fear that I would have to either spend money on coffees or be extremly annoyed that I would spend five minutes fixing my display even though technically, I would have done nothing wrong.
\newline 
In this scenario, deterrence theory can be used to explain how I stayed compliant to this particular rule or in another way, how it forced me and my co-workers to self-regulate ourselves out of fear of "punishment".
